How it works

The interpreter turns your words into a deal record, and a separate pricing engine prices that record from PagerDuty's public price book, checks it against an example approval policy, and routes it. The interpreter never sees a price. Anything the book can't price, or any term it can't read, goes to deal desk or back to the rep instead of being guessed.

1Interpret

Words to fields. Plan, users, stakeholders, billing, term, discount, payment terms, price lock, close date, events, AI Actions, add-ons. A term it can't read goes on an unparsed list.

2Price

Fields to dollars. Prices come only from data/price_book.json, and every line cites the entry it came from. A new price book changes prices without a code change.

3Check

Book and policy rules. Starter's 30-seat cap, stakeholders counting as seats, annual-upfront billing, bonus seats, the AI Actions promotion deadline, discount bands, payment terms, price locks.

4Route

Status and approvers. Ready, needs approval, blocked, or needs info. A quote could approve on its own only at 10% off or less with nothing but informational flags.

  • infoFor the record. Nothing to do.
  • checkA person confirms.
  • routeAdds an approver.
  • blockCan't be quoted as asked.
  • askThe rep needs to supply or restate something.

Blocked and needs-info quotes show no total, so nobody can forward a number that isn't real. This page runs the offline rules interpreter; the repo also has a Claude interpreter that fills the same record through a strict tool call.

Limits

  • The rules interpreter is a regex written with the test prompts in view. Unusual wording usually gets a request to restate rather than a price. The Claude interpreter is the intended path and hasn't been run against the test set yet.
  • The approval policy is an example. PagerDuty's real discount rules aren't public.
  • PagerDuty doesn't publish a price per credit, so quotes show credit quantities and leave the rate to deal desk.
  • Terms that change by year (ramps, stepped discounts) aren't modeled yet; the demo asks the rep instead.
  • No CRM write-back and no order form.
